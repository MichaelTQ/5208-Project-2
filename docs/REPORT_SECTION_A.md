# A: data preparation and cross-year/station comparisons

The project uses 2017–2024 five-minute station rainfall records from data.gov.sg. Apache Spark 3.5.8 performs full-data processing in the Asia/Singapore time zone. Raw values and source timestamps are retained; rainfall amounts use Decimal(20,9). Exact grid timestamps remain unchanged and records one second before a five-minute boundary move forward by one second. Compatible records are reduced to one deterministic representative; conflicting rainfall or coordinates are quarantined. No missing rainfall is imputed as zero. The final dataset contains 48,538,625 unique observations across 91 station IDs.

Availability varies substantially across stations and years. Calendar coverage uses 288 timestamp slots per day; observed-span coverage is reported separately. All stations have less than 90% calendar coverage in 2018, and the dataset has 63 entire days without valid observations across the study. These gaps prevent a direct interpretation of observed sums as complete annual rainfall.

A descriptive cohort of 20 stations was selected using at least 80% calendar coverage in every year and one recorded coordinate combination. Selection does not depend on rainfall ranking. For within-station annual comparisons, we retained month-day/time keys observed in all eight years, excluding February 29. Each station has 50,641–75,843 such keys; the combined panel contains 10,545,560 observations. Annual station means and wet fractions are averaged with equal station weights. This controls within-station calendar support, although support differs across stations and covers only 10–12 months with uneven weights.

On this matched support, 2023 has the highest mean observed rainfall rate (0.028935 mm per five-minute slot) and 2019 the lowest (0.017900). Rankings are sensitive to the observation support: the unbalanced network peaks in 2022, the fixed cohort at its available times in 2021, and the station-specific matched panel in 2023. These results describe the selected observations; they do not establish complete national annual totals or a long-term climate trend.

Requiring identical calendar support across all 20 stations and eight years leaves only 3,976 keys in five months; 70.7% are in September. This fails the pre-specified twelve-month condition for the primary annual comparison. On this restricted spatial panel, each station has 31,808 readings. S08 has the highest observed mean rate and S24 the lowest, but these rankings cannot be generalized to all seasons. The coordinate figure shows station points, without interpolation or area weighting.

Wet readings are defined strictly as rainfall greater than zero. Conditional wet p50/p90/p99 use Spark percentile_approx with accuracy 10,000; the reported approximation concerns rank, not missing-data uncertainty. Ten readings at least 20 mm per five minutes remain in the official cleaned data. Excluding these readings solely for sensitivity diagnostics reduces both sum and observation count. Only one occurs in the primary matched panel; the largest relative change in the equal-station mean is 0.0852%, and its year ranking is unchanged.

The interval-end interpretation (t−5 min, t] is an explicitly recorded research convention, not an officially confirmed timestamp definition. Historical revisions and update-field availability are unresolved, so results do not establish operational information availability. Independent checks verify source fingerprints, exact count/amount reconciliation, both matched panels against source observations, weighting and denominator rules, and station-year wet-quantile rank bounds. The descriptive cohort uses all years and must not be adopted automatically as a forecasting training selection.
